# LuxeInfra website blueprint

Planning draft · 11 October 2026 · Prepared for discussion before design and development

## 1. The central idea

**Beautiful spaces. Thoughtfully planned. Clearly delivered.**

LuxeInfra should combine the appeal of a design studio with the clarity of a well-organised project service. Visitors should feel: “They understand my taste, they can handle my work, and I know what happens next.”

The website’s distinguishing feature will be **Plan My Space**: one connected journey from choosing inspiration to describing requirements and requesting an estimate. The customer’s selected designs, rooms, services, uploaded plan, budget, and timeline become one project brief.

This is a proposed competitive advantage, not a claim that no competitor offers similar features. The advantage depends on how well the experience and follow-up are executed.

## 2. Preserve the theme; sharpen the design

Working assumption: preserve the live website’s ivory/white background, deep blue accents, elegant serif headlines, and premium residential imagery observed during the review. Confirm final colours against the exported site. Preserve the LuxeInfra logo and business identity.

Use warm ivory as the main canvas, deep blue for primary actions, dark ink for readable text, and restrained warm-grey borders. Avoid introducing a competing palette before reviewing the actual source assets.

Design rules:

- One strong idea and one main action per section.
- Large editorial headings, short supporting copy, generous spacing, and real project photographs.
- Use a clear sans-serif typeface for body text and forms; reserve the serif for headings.
- Let one beautiful image dominate the opening screen. Avoid rotating banners.
- Use subtle fades and hover responses; honour reduced-motion preferences.
- Use asymmetrical image-and-text compositions on desktop and simple vertical layouts on mobile.
- Keep room cards tactile and clean, with restrained rounded corners.
- Make touch targets generous, text readable, focus states visible, and form controls keyboard accessible.
- Do not rely on pale text, motion, autoplay video, or oversized empty areas to communicate luxury.

The desired feeling: a quiet, confident studio with useful tools—not a dense sales catalogue.

## 3. Brand voice and content direction

Voice: composed, warm, precise, and personal. Write about the homeowner’s daily life and the decisions being made. Avoid repeated “dream home,” “best interiors,” and unsupported superlatives.

Core headline: **A home that feels like you.**

Supporting brand line: **Designed around your life. Detailed around your needs.**

Useful recurring lines:

- “Start with a room. Or reimagine it all.”
- “Less guesswork. More clarity.”
- “Beautiful is personal. So is the plan.”
- “Your ideas, brought together.”
- “See the choices behind the finish.”

These are proposed copy directions. Operational claims, response times, prices, warranties, testimonials, and delivery commitments require business confirmation before publication.

## 4. Site structure

Primary navigation: **Spaces · Our Work · How It Works · About · Contact**

Persistent main action: **Plan My Space**

Spaces contains room inspiration and service information: kitchens, wardrobes and storage, living rooms, bedrooms, full-home interiors, and renovation where offered. Avoid creating an oversized menu with every trade.

Launch pages:

1. Homepage.
2. Plan My Space guided brief.
3. Spaces overview and priority room/service pages.
4. Our Work project index and individual case studies.
5. How It Works, including estimation and payment explanations.
6. About: real team, approach, service area, and contact details.
7. Contact and consultation request.
8. Privacy notice and relevant service/warranty terms based on actual practices.

Later additions: materials library, budget calculator, style discovery, selected location pages, and customer project tracking.

## 5. Homepage: complete content sequence

### A. Opening screen

Small label: **CONSIDERED INTERIORS. PERSONAL SPACES.**

Headline: **A home that feels like you.**

Body: “From a thoughtfully planned kitchen to a complete home, LuxeInfra brings your ideas, everyday needs, and personal style into one considered design.”

Primary button: **Plan My Space**

Secondary button: **Explore Our Work**

Small supporting line: “Choose your scope. Share your ideas. Request a tailored estimate.”

Visual: one strong, authentic completed project; an understated caption may identify its room, location, and scope when verified. Clearly label a concept render if one is used instead.

### B. Three easy starting points

Heading: **Where would you like to begin?**

Cards:

- **My whole home** — “Bring every room together.”
- **A room or a few upgrades** — “Focus on what matters most.”
- **I have a floor plan** — “Let’s start with your layout.”

All three open the same planner with suitable choices preselected. Floor-plan upload remains optional.

### C. Browse spaces

Heading: **Start with a room. Or reimagine it all.**

Show a concise selection of room cards. Each uses one image, one title, and one useful descriptor: “Kitchens made for your cooking routine,” “Storage with a place for everything,” or “Living rooms for everyday life.”

Use actual available catalogue counts only, or omit counts entirely.

### D. Signature work

Heading: **See the homes. Understand the choices.**

Show three verified case studies. Each card includes location, property type, and scope; approved budget ranges and completion duration can be added when available.

Button: **Inside This Project**

Avoid presenting stock imagery or AI renders as delivered work.

### E. Planning invitation

Heading: **Your floor plan is a starting point.**

Body: “Share your layout and tell us what you want to change. We’ll use your brief to discuss the scope, design direction, and next steps.”

Button: **Upload Plan & Start**

Alternative link: **Continue Without a Plan**

Do not promise instant plan analysis or an exact quote unless those capabilities exist and have been validated.

### F. The LuxeInfra approach

Heading: **Good design should feel clear.**

Three commitments to implement operationally before publishing:

- **A scope you understand** — Specify the rooms, work, and exclusions.
- **Choices you can compare** — Explain materials, finishes, and their trade-offs.
- **A process you can follow** — Show the next decision and project stage.

### G. Project process

Heading: **From first ideas to the final detail.**

Five stages: Share your brief → Discuss your space → Review design and quotation → Approve and execute → Handover and support.

Provide a short explanation of what the customer receives at each stage. Show payment milestones only after they are confirmed.

### H. Real client experiences

Use named, permission-approved testimonials, real project photos, and links to review sources where available. Avoid a rating number without supporting evidence.

### I. Helpful questions

Answer: Can you handle one room? Can I start without a floor plan? What affects my quotation? Which locations do you serve? What is included? How are changes handled? What does the warranty cover?

### J. Closing invitation and footer

Heading: **Let’s make room for your ideas.**

Body: “Tell us what you have in mind. We’ll help you shape the next step.”

Buttons: **Plan My Space** and **Talk to Our Team**.

Footer: actual service areas, business contact details, hours, real social links, and relevant policies. No placeholder links.

## 6. Plan My Space: the main customer journey

Use a dedicated, mobile-friendly page with a progress indicator, back navigation, and a compact editable summary. No account is required to begin. Keep contact details near the end. Preserve non-sensitive choices when moving between steps.

### Step 1 — Tell us about your space

Ask for city or pincode, apartment/independent home/other, home configuration where relevant, approximate area, and whether it is a new home or renovation. Label area clearly, preferably carpet area, and provide “Not sure.” Avoid requiring a precise street address at this stage.

Service-area rules should give an honest outcome: serviced, needs confirmation, or outside current coverage. Never silently accept an unavailable service as confirmed.

### Step 2 — Choose the work

Start with Full home / Selected rooms / Specific work / Help me decide.

Separate **where the work happens** from **what work is required**. Selecting “bedroom” does not automatically mean all bedroom services are included.

Potential work options, subject to actual offering: modular kitchen, wardrobes/storage, TV unit, false ceiling, lighting, painting, flooring/tiling, electrical, plumbing, bathroom renovation, furniture, and design consultation.

For each selected room, let users choose relevant work. Keep specialist quantities optional until designer review. Avoid a long, technical bill-of-quantities questionnaire.

### Step 3 — Share your direction

Show a small curated image set for style and finish preferences. Include “Help me choose.” Carry in designs saved from the gallery and allow optional reference images or a written note.

Ask one useful lifestyle question: “What should your home do better?” Options might include storage, easier upkeep, more comfortable family time, work-from-home space, or better use of a small room.

### Step 4 — Plan, budget, and timing

Accept PDF/JPG/PNG floor plans and room photos with explicit size limits. Allow a clear skip path. Ask for a comfortable budget band, including “Need guidance,” property readiness, and desired start window.

Keep uploaded plans private, accessible only to the authorised team, with a defined retention practice. Validate file types and sizes, show upload progress, and provide retry/remove controls.

### Step 5 — Review and request

Show the full editable brief. Ask for name, phone, preferred contact method/time, and optional email unless delivery requires it. Explain why contact details are collected; keep promotional consent separate from the project enquiry.

Primary button: **Request My Estimate**.

Confirmation headline: **Your project brief is with us.**

Show a reference number, summary, and a realistic agreed response window. Offer a copy of the brief and a consultation request as next steps. Never show success before the request is safely stored.

### Initial estimate versus instant calculator

Launch with a **designer-reviewed estimate request**. An upload does not create an exact price automatically.

Introduce instant indicative ranges after LuxeInfra supplies tested pricing rules for quantities, materials, finishes, hardware, location, and scope. Show assumptions and inclusions beside the number. Identify excluded civil work, appliances, furniture, taxes, or other items where applicable.

Avoid one generic rate per square foot for every project. Prevent double counting when full-home packages overlap with separately selected services. Explain that measured quantities and approved specifications determine the final quotation.

## 7. Distinctive features worth developing

**My Space Brief:** selected designs and requirements remain connected throughout the journey. Clicking “Use This as Inspiration” attaches the actual design reference to the brief.

**A quote that explains itself:** room-level scope, material specifications, inclusions, exclusions, optional upgrades, and quotation validity. Let clients see why a price changes.

**Design for everyday life:** inspiration is tagged by practical needs—easy upkeep, compact spaces, extra storage, family living—as well as visual style.

**The story behind a room:** a project case study explains the initial problem, design choices, work completed, and result. More useful than a gallery of attractive images alone.

**Plan in phases:** later, help customers distinguish must-have work from upgrades that can wait. A designer must review sequencing so a later phase does not require undoing earlier work.

**Compare finishes simply:** later, show a small number of supported alternatives with care needs, appearance, and pricing implications. Use actual supplier specifications.

Build the brief and transparent quotation first. A public 3D planner, automated floor-plan reader, and customer portal are later investments that need a demonstrated business case.

## 8. Supporting pages

**Room/service page:** strong image → who it is for → layout/options → relevant material choices → verified examples → scope and exclusions → FAQs → prefilled Plan My Space button.

**Case study:** client brief → before/after where available → floor-plan decisions → room photographs → materials → scope → approved budget/duration → testimonial → “Plan a Similar Project.”

**About:** why LuxeInfra exists, real team, approach, service geography, and how responsibility is managed. Replace generic brand paragraphs with specifics.

**How It Works:** customer actions, team deliverables, approvals, payments, change requests, handover, and support.

**Contact:** working phone/WhatsApp links, actual location, opening hours, preferred callback request, and links to the project planner. Phone and WhatsApp should use consistent international-format numbers.

## 9. What happens behind the form

Every request should create a stored enquiry with its brief, file references, contact preference, source page, selected inspirations, and timestamp. Notify the designated team, but retain the enquiry even if the notification fails.

Minimum follow-up stages: New → Contacted → Consultation planned → Estimate sent → Won / Not proceeding.

Include spam protection, duplicate handling, failed-submission recovery, upload access controls, and a visible success state. Define who owns follow-up. A polished form cannot compensate for unanswered enquiries.

Measure planner starts, completed steps, successful submissions, consultation requests, WhatsApp clicks, and qualified leads. Do not send names, phone numbers, addresses, or floor-plan contents into analytics events.

## 10. Build roadmap and completion gates

| Stage | Work | Completion gate |
|---|---|---|
| 0. Recover and audit | Obtain the correct live project source, back it up, inspect hosting and connected services, reconcile factual claims | The current site runs in a review environment and its dependencies are understood |
| 1. Content and structure | Confirm service areas, offerings, authentic project assets, tone, navigation, and all planner questions | Approved sitemap, content inventory, and planner flow |
| 2. Visual direction | Design homepage, planner, gallery, and case study for mobile and desktop | Approved representative screens and component styles |
| 3. First working release | Build core pages, work selection, private uploads, enquiry storage, team notification, and design-to-brief links | A test enquiry reaches the correct team with its complete brief and files |
| 4. Verification and launch | Test accessibility, mobile behaviour, routes, forms, uploads, SEO basics, redirects, domain, and publishing/rollback process | Review passes and business owner approves the live release |
| 5. Optimise | Assess real enquiries and drop-off; improve confusing questions and follow-up | Changes are based on observed customer behaviour |
| 6. Expand | Add a validated cost calculator, finish comparison, style discovery, or project tracker | Pricing/service data and team capacity support the feature |

Do not commit to a launch date until the source, assets, integrations, and content readiness have been assessed. Changes to hosting and the public domain happen after a working preview and launch checks.

## 11. First release scope

Must have: refined homepage; correct navigation; room/service pages; authentic case studies where assets exist; Plan My Space; optional floor-plan upload; working lead storage and notifications; contact/WhatsApp; service areas; confirmed process/FAQ content; privacy information; mobile and accessibility checks.

Next: indicative estimator using real rates; richer filters; saved shortlists; material comparisons; style discovery; useful location pages with real local evidence.

Later: customer portal, project tracking, advanced 3D, automatic floor-plan interpretation, and payment collection.

Branding requirement: the delivered public site should display LuxeInfra branding without an “Edit with Lovable” badge, using the appropriate hosting setting or an independently deployed codebase.

## 12. Information needed from LuxeInfra

- Correct project source and authorised access to connected services.
- Logo and any existing brand guidelines.
- Actual city/service coverage and minimum project scope, if any.
- Confirmed services, design fees, consultation terms, and payment process.
- Real project photos, permission to publish them, and project facts.
- Verified testimonials, business figures, and rating sources.
- Supported materials, hardware, warranty terms, and delivery conditions.
- The person/team responsible for enquiries and realistic response availability.
- Rates and package definitions before developing an instant calculator.

During the earlier site review, the homepage showed conflicting home counts and design counts that differed from the design index. Resolve these before reusing them. Audit gallery routing and placeholder links as part of the source review.

## 13. How success will be judged

The principal outcome is **more qualified project conversations**, measured with actual follow-up outcomes—not merely more button clicks.

Track planner completion, qualified enquiry share, time to first response, consultations booked, estimates sent, and booked projects. Establish a baseline before setting numeric targets. Review visitor questions and abandoned steps to identify unnecessary friction.

Launch acceptance includes: completed briefs survive reload/submission failures appropriately; uploads remain private; mobile users can finish the flow; contact links work; each page has a clear next step; all public facts are verified; and the team can retrieve and act on an enquiry.
